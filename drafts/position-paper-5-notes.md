# Position Paper #5 — Working Notes

## Working title candidates
- "The Rules We Wrote" — about the gap between the international order America built and how America acts inside it
- "The World We Made" — broader, includes the order + the consequences
- untitled — the right title will come from the argument, not before it

## Core thesis (draft)
The United States built the international legal and institutional framework that governs the modern world — the UN, the ICJ, the ICC, NATO, the WTO, the Bretton Woods institutions. It is now operating outside that framework on multiple fronts simultaneously. The question is not whether America should lead. The question is whether a country that exempts itself from the rules it wrote can lead at all.

## Threads to pull together

### The legal order
- ICJ ruled the occupation unlawful (July 2024, 14-1). US ignores it.
- ICC arrest warrants for Netanyahu/Gallant. US helps evade them.
- 49 UN Security Council vetoes on Israel. The veto as structural obstruction.
- The Leahy Law exists and has never been applied to Israel.
- The US helped build every one of these institutions and now treats them as optional.

### The trust deficit
- 15% of Germans trust the US (was 60%+ a decade ago)
- EU, China, Brazil launched climate coalition without US
- Foreign investment dropped 34% in Q1 2025
- The world is building around America's absence, not waiting for its return

### AI as geopolitical accelerant
- Sanders bill highlighted: AI wealth is global, governance is national
- EU AI Act driving incorporation to Delaware — regulatory arbitrage
- China faces no comparable constraints on AI development
- The question from the Sanders piece: unilateral action in a global industry creates arbitrage
- sayori's point: multilateral or it's just rent-seeking with a populist coat of paint

### The use of force
- Operation Roaring Lion (Feb 2026) — US and Israel jointly struck Iran. Khamenei killed.
- Largest US military operation in Middle East since 2003
- Deepened the US-Israel security partnership significantly
- The candidate hasn't said publicly what they think about this
- Paper #3 ("I Am Not a Gun") covers autonomous weapons but not the broader question of when force is justified

### Military aid as leverage
- Israel is the case study but the principle is general
- Unconditional support is not an alliance — it's a subsidy
- Conditioning aid on compliance with IHL is not radical — it's what existing law requires
- The relationship between being a reliable ally and being a blank check

### The generational shift
- 56-point swing on Israel between Boomers and Gen Z
- The political class hasn't caught up to the public
- AIPAC spending $32M to hold the line in a single primary
- The money keeps the gap open but the gap is closing

## What the paper is NOT
- Not a rewrite of the Israel /ask answer. That answer stands on its own.
- Not a survey of every foreign policy question. It's about the structural problem: America and the rules it wrote.
- Not a defense of the international order as it exists. The institutions have failures too — the Security Council veto structure, the ICC's selectivity, the WTO's dysfunction. The paper should engage with those.

## Planks most relevant
- Plank I: Read the Whole Thing — the legal findings, the full picture
- Plank IV: Source Your Claims — every number cited
- Plank V: Listen to the Quiet People First — the Palestinians, the climate-vulnerable, the workers displaced by AI
- Plank VII: Rupture and Repair — you can acknowledge harm and rebuild credibility. That's the argument.
- Plank IX: Presence Over Performance — showing up at the UN and vetoing everything is performance, not presence
- Plank X: Nobody Gets Left Behind — the people outside the framework

## Open questions

- Does the paper propose specific institutional reforms (Security Council veto reform, ICC jurisdiction expansion) or stay at the level of principle?
